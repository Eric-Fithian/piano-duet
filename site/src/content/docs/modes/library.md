---
title: Library
description: Import MIDI or MusicXML, or turn a piano video into a score, then play it with a cursor that waits for you.
---

**Library** holds full songs. Import a MIDI (`.mid`) or MusicXML (`.musicxml`, `.xml`, `.mxl`) file with **Import**, by dropping it on the list, or with **Import file** on a catalogue entry, which links to the composer's official store. MuseScore exports both formats; PDF scores are not supported yet. One time signature per piece is supported. Rhythms are written on sixteenths or, in simple meters, triplet eighths, chosen per beat; finer values such as 32nds and sextuplets are rounded onto them. MusicXML keeps the written staves; a two-track MIDI file maps its lower track to the left hand, and other MIDI files split at middle C. Songs and plays stay in `.local/learner.sqlite3`. Open a song and press **Space**: the cursor waits for each note or chord, and if your last three attacks match another passage it moves there. Sections of eight bars (four in short pieces) are coloured by recent first-try accuracy; click one to jump. **▶** previews from the cursor. Pausing or finishing saves the play; the list shows plays, recent and best first-try accuracy, how much of the piece you have covered, and when you last played it. Song plays do not award XP yet.

## Editing a song

Transcriptions can get the tempo, meter, key or bar lines wrong, or mishear notes. Open a song and choose **Edit** to see the whole score with a toolbar. **Tempo** rewrites the playing speed without moving any written note; **Meter** and **Key** change the time and key signatures; **Bar lines ◀ ▶** move every bar line a step (an eighth in /8 meters, otherwise a quarter) earlier or later, for when the downbeat is in the wrong place. Click a note to select it: **Delete** removes it, **↑/↓** move it a semitone (with **⇧**, an octave) and **←/→** select the previous or next note. Double-click a staff to add a note at that line or space and moment, spelled from the key signature and lasting until the next note on that staff (at most a beat). **Undo** (or ⌘Z) steps back, **Cancel** discards the changes, **Save** keeps them, and **Revert to transcription** restores the original, which is kept from the first edit on. Plays are tied to the notes they were played against: retiming, rebarring or rekeying keeps a song's play history, while adding, removing or repitching notes starts a fresh one, and reverting brings the original's plays back.

## From YouTube

**From YouTube** turns a single video of up to 15 minutes into a Library song. yt-dlp downloads only the audio into a temporary folder under `.cache/youtube`, deleted as soon as it is decoded with the bundled ffmpeg (from `imageio-ffmpeg`, independent of Homebrew); downloading may conflict with YouTube's terms, so use it for personal practice. **Transkun 2.0** (Yan & Duan, 2024; shipped with its checkpoint in the `transkun` package) transcribes the audio on the Mac GPU at about 5% of real time. On synthetic renders of ASAP performances it found 99% of note onsets against 93% for the live microphone model; on a real recording the two agreed on 93% of notes, so the gain on real audio is smaller than the synthetic figure. It passes key-down durations, without the sustain pedal, to **MIDI2ScoreTransformer** (Beyer & Dai, ISMIR 2024) writes the score: bar lines, rhythm, key, and which hand plays each note. On the ASAP test pieces its onset error is 15.6% versus 47.9% for MuseScore's MIDI import. It runs on the CPU, about 50× faster than real time on an M4 Pro (MPS is roughly 60× slower for its step-by-step decoding), in a separate environment under `.cache/m2st` that `uv run --frozen piano-duet setup` installs with pinned versions and a checksummed checkpoint. The repository has no licence file; it is used locally for personal practice. The score uses the meter covering most bars, with bar lines aligned after a pickup, and a tempo measured from the performance. Solo piano recordings work best.
