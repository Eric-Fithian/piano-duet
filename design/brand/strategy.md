# Piano Duet: brand strategy

## Positioning

**Real music, freshly written for your piano, privately on your device.** Piano Duet combines readable notation rather than falling-note prompts, a fresh score for each Reading exercise, on-device listening and generation, and a free, open-source product. Each is a concrete reason to choose it: the notation builds a transferable skill, fresh music rewards reading rather than memorisation, local models keep audio on the device, and openness makes the product inspectable and accessible without a subscription. These are product promises, not a claim that the models can assess every musical skill.

The target personality is **musical, lucid, encouraging, curious and quietly playful**. Warmth appears most strongly after an earned milestone, while the practice surface stays composed.

## The people and the job

For people learning or returning to piano who want useful practice between lessons, on an acoustic or digital piano they already own. It also serves more experienced players building reading fluency or technique. It is not a replacement for a teacher: it can observe notes and timing, but cannot judge all the musical choices a teacher hears.

The job is to make the next few minutes at the piano clear, manageable and worth repeating: give me music at an appropriate challenge, listen locally, tell me specifically what happened, and help me see how practice adds up. On a phone at the piano, the score is the product. Trust comes from making the model's limits and the privacy of audio legible, not from making the technology the hero. ## Personality and voice

- **Encouraging, not ingratiating:** acknowledge effort without telling people they are amazing for every action.
- **Musically literate, not academic:** use familiar terms such as bar, hand and tempo; explain a less familiar term in context.
- **Clear, not clinical:** describe the next action and the evidence behind feedback, without a wall of metrics.
- **Playful, not arcade-like:** a sense of momentum and small discoveries, without combat metaphors or visual noise.
- **Honest, not judgmental:** distinguish a missed note from a possible recognition error and let the player review or exclude it.

Use sentence case, direct verbs and a warm, matter-of-fact tone. Avoid exclamation marks as a default, shame, and invented claims about mastery.

| Moment | Example | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Button | “Start playing” / “Hear the score” | Names the action in musical terms. |
| Empty library | “Your library is empty. Import a score or recording to begin.” | Explains what belongs here and how to start. |
| Useful feedback | “Two notes needed another try. Review the score.” | Specific, actionable and non-punitive. |
| Uncertain recognition | “We couldn't confidently hear that take. Try again or review what was heard.” | Does not turn model uncertainty into player failure. |
| Progress | “Stage 3 complete. Next: skips.” | Celebrates an earned change and names what comes next. |

## What gamified means

Make progress visible through an understandable stage path, recent attempts, meaningful skill goals, drill levels and optional XP. Five visible noteheads are a window onto the existing 24-stage Reading curriculum, not a five-stage curriculum. The next goal should connect to a real musical capability; XP is a measure of participation, separate from evidence of skill. Explain assistance and scoring before it matters. Celebrate a stage or level with a brief, dismissible or self-clearing moment and a concrete next step; quiet feedback after an ordinary take. Never interrupt a performance, obscure notation during play, or use sound/confetti by default. Respect reduced motion. No loss streaks, daily-pressure streaks, leaderboards, manipulative reminders, loot, punitive language, or false mastery claims. A missed day carries no penalty.

## Name recommendation — decision belongs to the user

Keep **Piano Duet** for this phase. The name can express two voices: the player makes music and the on-device listener responds with notation, feedback and the next challenge. The two-voice monogram embodies that relationship. The enabled modes focus on individual practice, so “Duet” can still suggest live accompaniment; test that expectation with new users. The positioning line is “Real music. Fresh every time.” A name change remains the user's decision; none is proposed here.
