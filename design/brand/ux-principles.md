# UX principles: the score is the page

## The real progress model

Reading has **24 stages**, not five. A stage is a curriculum step such as First notes or Steps and skips, with a defined musical scope and evidence requirements. The current stage is the first unearned step. The path in the prototype shows a *window* of five of those 24 stage noteheads; it is not a five-stage curriculum or a five-take meter. A checked gold notehead means a stage is complete; a heavier outline identifies the current stage. The full path and the evidence behind a stage belong in Progress.

For stages 1–11 the curriculum requires three recent eligible fresh scores per relevant hand; stages 12–17 require four and stages 18–24 require six, with grand-staff stages assessed as a combined group. Each group needs at least 90% first-try pitch accuracy, and rhythm stages also require at least 80% rhythm with sufficient evidence; later stages have minimum tempos. Key and meter coverage vary by stage. The first two stages also require sufficient accurate evidence for the foundation notes. Excluded, non-microphone and duplicate-task evidence is not counted, and the exact readiness calculation is in [`reading-stage-requirements.ts`](../../src/core/reading-stage-requirements.ts) and [`reading-curriculum.ts`](../../src/core/reading-curriculum.ts). Stage completion is evidence-based; it is not a daily streak or a count of clean takes.

Reader **level and XP are a separate reward track**. Stage 1 awards 10 XP for each first-try note, 2 for a recovered note, a streak bonus for at least four consecutive first-try attack groups, and other applicable bonuses; help halves the subtotal. A new-note introduction earns three times the stage note value (30 XP at stage 1) and is not assessed as a regular score. Clearing stage 1 adds 300 XP, with each subsequent stage clear worth 100 more than the previous one. Level 1 needs 400 XP; each later level needs 100 more. Excluded attempts recalculate the total. See [`reading-rewards.ts`](../../src/core/reading-rewards.ts) and [`reading_curriculum.py`](../../src/piano_duet/reading_curriculum.py). XP is encouragement, not a proficiency claim.

A Reading streak counts consecutive first-try **attacks**; notes sounded together count as one attack group. The topline shows a positive best run after a take, never a zero or a broken streak, and hides counters during play. The beamed motif grows from one to four noteheads; longer runs retain four and show the exact count in text. Drills have their own per-drill levels and personal-best steady tempo: three clean takes in a row establish a steady tempo for that drill configuration, credited at the slowest of the three. Library has no Reading or Drills progress display. These are separate measures, not interchangeable levels.

The result frame is an **illustrative fixture**: a regular, non-introduction 16-attack C/D/E stage-1 score with 15 first-try attacks, one recovered attack at bar 2 beat 1, and a best run of 11. At stage 1 this yields 150 first-try XP + 2 recovered XP + 55 streak XP + 300 stage-clear XP = **507 XP**. A hypothetical previous 600 XP gives 1,107 total: level 3, 207/600 into the level (the initial frame shows level 2, 200/500). Previous eligible scores and foundation-note evidence are assumed to have already met the other stage requirements. The fixture is rendered by the real ScoreView and VexFlow; the example progress and performance marks are static, not evidence saved to the app.

## Layout and controls

Use the selected **topline** on all sizes. On portrait phones, mode, stage/level, Settings and score options sit above the page, with Listen, a mic indicator and Start/Stop below. The reserved status slot in the transport stays the same height before and during a take; short expected/heard text is at least 18 px. Listen is at least 12 px from Start/Stop, with the microphone state and level between them. On short landscape screens the same controls fit in one 44 px topline, leaving the score's width available for a four-bar line. No score control floats over notation: expand the score-view controls and options in the chrome, growing it when needed.

A phone portrait score uses two bars per line; portrait iPad uses balanced two-bar lines and landscape has all four bars on one line when the music fits. The current score fits without vertical scrolling in these examples. The next exercise is a single “Next score ready” label rather than a second score or an invitation to scroll. The prototype's injected engraving spacing and viewport-specific line cap are review fixtures; production needs responsive engraving that retains readable notes for denser scores rather than silently clipping them.

The cursor is the same soft outlined column at rest and in play, inserted behind the current note and staff, after the latest accepted attack. It must not cover clefs, meter or finger numbers. Fingering belongs outside the notehead on the side away from the stem. A correct note has a ✓ as well as green; a wrong note has an ink ring and × plus a descriptive expected/heard status. The result preserves the take's marks, lives below the score, and offers a 44 px Review button, Next score and Try again. A filled gold notehead celebrates the earned stage; XP remains ordinary ink text. The gold fill animates briefly, with the same static end state for reduced motion.

## Containers and appearance

Notation reaches the safe-area edges without a card or decorative gutter. Boundaries are for controls, dialogs and menus; lists use alignment, whitespace and rules. Use 4 px corners for controls, reserving pills for status badges. Drills has a labelled 44 px exercise picker and puts explanation behind Help; Library uses whole-row targets with one trailing icon; Models gives every row a test action that names the download and size when needed.

Follow the operating system by default, with System / Light / Dark in Settings and a locally remembered override. Dark uses dim warm paper; the optional Night page uses warm light ink, heavier staff strokes and a cursor edge at least 4.5:1 against the page. Appearance controls and the depicted playing, results, score-view changes and progress are prototype examples only. They do not implement new scoring, microphone recognition, count-in or saved preferences.
